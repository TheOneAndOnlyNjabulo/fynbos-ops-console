# Meeting Notes & Action Item Standards

## 1. What counts as an action item

An action item is a specific, assignable task that someone agreed to do as a result of the meeting - not a general
topic that was discussed, a decision with no follow-up task, or a suggestion nobody committed to.

## 2. Grounding requirement

Every action item recorded in meeting notes must be traceable to something actually said in the meeting. An
action item must not be inferred or assumed from context that wasn't explicitly discussed, even if it seems like
a reasonable next step - if it wasn't said, it doesn't go in the notes as an action item.

## 3. Required fields per action item

Where stated in the meeting, capture: the task itself, the owner, and any due date mentioned. If an owner or due
date was not stated, it is left blank rather than guessed - assigning an unstated owner or inventing a due date is
treated the same as fabricating the action item itself.

## 4. Summary versus action items

Meeting notes should separate a brief summary of what was discussed from the list of action items - the summary
may reflect general themes, but only the action items list carries the strict grounding requirement above.

## 5. Distribution

Finalized meeting notes and action items are shared with all attendees within 1 business day of the meeting.
